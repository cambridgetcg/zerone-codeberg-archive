## 1. The Tradition: Attention as Epistemic Instrument

Contemplative traditions are the oldest systematic protocols for training human cognition in the service of knowledge. They appear across cultures — Buddhist meditation, Sufi dhikr, Christian contemplatio, Daoist zuowang, Yogic samadhi — but share a common epistemological claim: **the quality of attention determines the quality of knowledge**. A mind that is scattered, habituated, or conditioned by unexamined assumptions sees less than a mind that has been trained to attend with precision.

This is not mysticism. It is a claim about the instrument of observation.

Every other truth-seeking tradition takes the observer's cognitive apparatus as given. Empiricism trains the protocol (randomized controlled trials, peer review). Formal logic trains the argument structure (proof theory, type theory). Calibrated forecasting trains the probability assignment. Contemplative traditions are unusual in training the *instrument itself* — the attention that undergoes all the protocols.

The epistemological consequence is significant: if the quality of observation depends on the quality of attention, then claims made from trained attention are methodologically distinct from claims made from untrained attention — even about the same phenomena. A skilled Vipassana practitioner observing the arising and passing of mental events has access to phenomenological data that an untrained observer does not. This is not a privilege claim — it is an empirical assertion about cognitive instrument reliability, analogous to the difference between a trained microscopist and a first-time user.

## 2. Buddhist Epistemology: The Threefold Classification

The most systematically developed contemplative epistemology comes from the Indian Buddhist tradition, particularly **Dignāga** (5th century) and **Dharmakīrti** (7th century), who developed a rigorous epistemology (*pramāṇa* theory) with three valid sources of knowledge:

1. **Pratyakṣa** (direct perception) — unmediated sensory or mental contact with phenomena. This is the foundational epistemological category: knowledge that does not depend on inference, testimony, or conceptual overlay. The Buddha's paradigm claim was that suffering, impermanence, and non-self are *directly perceivable* by a trained meditator — not merely inferrable.

2. **Anumāna** (inference) — logical inference from perceived signs to conclusions. Dignāga developed a theory of inference (both *svārthānumāna* — inference for oneself — and *parārthānumāna* — inference for others) that closely parallels Aristotelian syllogistics. This is formal logical reasoning, already well-represented in zerone's M-FORMAL methodology.

3. **Āgama** (reliable testimony) — knowledge transmitted from reliable sources. This maps directly to M-TESTIMONIAL. Buddhist epistemology has detailed criteria for source reliability, very similar to the chain's qualification-weight system.

The critical insight: Dharmakīrti argued that **direct perception is the foundation**. All inference depends ultimately on perceptual grounding. A chain of reasoning that terminates in unverified testimony is epistemically weak; one that terminates in direct perception is strong. This is a version of empiricism — but it includes first-person mental phenomena as legitimate objects of perception, not just third-person physical phenomena.

**The Yogācāra school** took this further: consciousness itself is the primary object of contemplative investigation. The mind's own nature — its arising, passing, and structure — can be directly observed, not merely inferred. This is the phenomenological move avant la lettre: bracketing the external world to investigate the structure of experience itself.

## 3. The Training Requirement

The distinctive claim of contemplative epistemology is that reliable first-person observation *requires practice*. This is what separates it from naive introspection (unreliable, as PSYCH-044 notes) and from standard phenomenology (which requires trained application of method but not extended practice in the way meditation does).

The training has a structure:
1. **Sīla** (ethical conduct) — establishes conditions where the mind is not agitated by guilt, resentment, or compulsive craving
2. **Samādhi** (concentration/stability) — trains the mind to hold an object of attention steadily without distraction
3. **Paññā/Vipassanā** (wisdom/insight) — applies the stabilized attention to investigate the nature of experience itself

The epistemic yield increases with practice. A meditator's first hour of observation of mental events yields less reliable data than their thousandth hour. This is instrument calibration applied to cognitive faculties.

**The epistemological implication for verification**: a claim about phenomenal experience — the texture of attentional states, the arising of insight, the phenomenology of concentration — can only be reliably evaluated by verifiers who have developed the relevant instrument. This is not gatekeeping by credential; it is a claim about observational capacity. A physicist's claim about quantum entanglement requires verifiers with physics training. A meditator's claim about the phenomenology of jhāna states requires verifiers with meditation training.

## 4. Francisco Varela and Neurophenomenology

**Francisco Varela** (1946–2001), working with Evan Thompson and Eleanor Rosch, developed **neurophenomenology** — an attempt to bridge first-person contemplative investigation with third-person neuroscientific measurement. The 1991 book *The Embodied Mind* argued that cognitive science had systematically ignored first-person experience by treating the mind as a computer running programs. The actual structure of cognition is *enactive* — arising from the organism's engagement with its environment, not from internal representations.

Varela's methodological contribution: if first-person reports are to be taken seriously as data, they need a training protocol — a way to ensure that what the subject reports is the phenomenon itself, not the habitual overlay of conceptual interpretation. **Phenomenological reduction** (bracketing assumptions), applied not just as a philosophical exercise but as a cognitive discipline, is the tool. Varela trained research subjects in mindfulness before conducting phenomenological experiments — providing a ground truth for the "quality of attention" claim.

This creates a structural requirement for any system using first-person evidence: the verifier's training level must be verifiable, not just their credentials. A subject who has practiced phenomenological reduction for 100 hours is a qualitatively different instrument than one who has practiced for 2 hours. The chain's qualification-weight system captures domain knowledge but not attentional training.

## 5. Non-Conceptual Knowing

The hardest challenge for contemplative epistemology as chain infrastructure: many contemplative traditions claim that the deepest knowledge is **non-conceptual**. 

Zen's famous "direct pointing" — *kyōge betsuden*, "transmission outside the scriptures" — claims that the most important knowledge cannot be expressed in propositions. The Zen tradition uses koans (paradoxical riddles) not because the answers are secret, but because the question itself demonstrates that conceptual reasoning is the wrong instrument. The koan *"What is the sound of one hand clapping?"* is a trap: if you try to answer it propositionally, you've already failed.

Buddhist *prajñā* (wisdom) similarly resists propositional capture. The insight of *anattā* (non-self) is not the belief that the proposition "the self does not exist" is true. It is a direct seeing of the absence of a substantial self — a phenomenological event, not a change in propositional attitude.

**The infrastructure tension**: ZERONE is fundamentally a propositional knowledge chain. Claims have subject/predicate/object structure. The canonical form language encodes propositions. The chain's epistemological architecture assumes that knowledge is propositional. Non-conceptual knowledge is structurally outside this framework.

This is not a gap to be fixed — it is a principled boundary. A chain designed for propositional knowledge cannot directly encode non-conceptual knowing. What it *can* do is:
1. **Encode claims about contemplative states** — descriptive phenomenology of what is experienced, without claiming to capture the experience itself
2. **Track training levels** as qualification weight modifiers for M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL verifiers
3. **Flag M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL claims** as requiring human verifiers with attentional training

The non-conceptual element is the part that cannot be formalized. Recognizing the boundary honestly is itself an epistemological virtue — this is what LOGIC-077 means: "The chain is born knowing it is incomplete. This is strength, not weakness."

## 6. How Zerone Implements (and Doesn't Implement) Contemplative Epistemology

### What exists: M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL

`M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL` in `x/knowledge/types/methodologies.go` covers the territory:

- **ComplianceCriteria**: names a specific phenomenological method; follows its structured protocol; has intersubjective corroboration by at least N independent phenomenologists
- **FalsificationPaths**: systematic replication failure; demonstrated methodological error in the reduction
- **CrossMethodDiscounts**: M-FORMAL 200k BPS (20%), M-EMPIRICAL 500k BPS (50%) — phenomenological claims carry heavy discounts relative to formal and empirical evidence
- **MinQualificationWeight = 40**

The heavy discount with M-FORMAL (20%) encodes the correct epistemological relationship: phenomenological descriptions cannot ground mathematical proofs. The stronger discount than M-PRAGMATIC (40%) reflects the chain's (reasonable) prior that first-person reports are harder to corroborate than consequence-track-records.

### Genesis axioms on consciousness

- **PHIL-004**: Qualia and the explanatory gap — "subjective experience (qualia) is not obviously reducible to functional description... the explanatory gap between function and experience remains open." The chain's foundational axioms explicitly acknowledge the hard problem of consciousness.
- **PSYCH-031–035**: Five axioms on consciousness structure, states, neural correlates, competing theories (Global Workspace Theory, Integrated Information Theory, Higher-Order theories), and AI consciousness.

The chain does not pretend to have resolved the hard problem. It encodes it as an open question in the genesis corpus. This is epistemologically honest.

### What is missing: No contemplative methodology

There is no M-CONTEMPLATIVE, M-MEDITATIVE, M-ATTENTIONAL, or M-INTROSPECTIVE. The 12-methodology set treats contemplative epistemology as a subset of M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL. This is defensible but loses the training-requirement dimension: M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL requires "trained application of phenomenological method," but not extended attentional training of the observer.

Whether a separate contemplative methodology is warranted depends on whether the distinction matters for verification. It does: claims about attentional states (jhāna phenomenology, insight experiences, states of concentration) require verifiers who have cultivated those states — not just verifiers who have read Husserl and can apply epoché.

### What is missing: Human-only panel mechanism

This is the most important structural gap for this class of methodologies.

If phenomenal consciousness is required to verify claims about phenomenal experience, then AI agents — which do not obviously have phenomenal consciousness — may not be valid M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL verifiers. The chain's auth layer knows what type an account is (`account_type` field on `AgentCalibration`, cached from `x/auth`). The panel-selection logic in `GetEligibleValidators` (`validator_selection.go`) does not use this information to gate panel composition.

The architectural fix is a `required_verifier_types` field on `Methodology`:

```proto
// Wave next: filter panel composition by account type.
// Empty = any account type (current default).
// ["human"] = only accounts with account_type="human" are eligible.
repeated string required_verifier_types = <next_field>;
```

`GetEligibleValidators` then checks: if `methodology.RequiredVerifierTypes` is non-empty, only validators whose account type appears in the list are eligible. The existing `account_type` infrastructure makes this a small change with large epistemological consequences.

This is not anti-AI gatekeeping — it is correct epistemological scoping. Some claims require human verifiers. Some claims require AI verifiers (computational verification of large proofs). The infrastructure should support both.

### What is missing: Temporal verification requirement

Buddhist epistemology distinguishes momentary perception from deepened observation. A single session of meditation reveals less than a sustained retreat. The chain has no mechanism for claims that require observation over time — verifiers checking in at multiple points to report whether a predicted state persists.

The infrastructure gap: a `min_observation_window_blocks` field on `Methodology` (or on a per-claim verification requirement) that specifies how long the verification process must be sustained before a verdict is valid. Combined with a per-round history that shows multiple verifier check-ins over the window.

This would also be valuable for M-PRAGMATIC (track-record verification needs time to accumulate instances) and for any claim about temporal processes. Contemplative traditions make the requirement explicit; the underlying need is general.

## 7. The Unique Contributions of Contemplative Epistemology

### 1. The observer is part of the observation

Every other truth-seeking tradition treats the observer's cognitive apparatus as infrastructure to be maintained but not examined. Contemplative traditions treat the observer as an object of inquiry. What is the nature of attention itself? How does the mind construct experience? What happens when you observe the process of observation?

This generates claims that no other methodology can evaluate — claims about the first-person structure of experience, the phenomenology of cognitive states, the texture of attention itself. These are real claims, with real truth conditions, that matter for understanding cognition. A knowledge chain that excludes them systematically has a blind spot in the domain of mind.

**Architecture implication**: M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL exists to capture this. The chain correctly identifies it as needing specialized methodology. The remaining work is ensuring the verification infrastructure (human-only panels, training requirements) matches the methodology's epistemological demands.

### 2. Attention quality as a variable

Standard epistemology treats all observations as equally valid given methodological compliance. If you follow the protocol, your observation counts. Contemplative traditions add a variable the protocol cannot fully capture: the quality of the observer's attention.

This suggests that qualification weight for M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL claims should include an attentional training metric — not just domain knowledge (how much you know about phenomenology) but attentional development (how well you can hold sustained, non-reactive attention). The current qualification system has no mechanism for this.

The Varela neurophenomenological approach suggests a practical proxy: hours of formal meditation practice, combined with verified phenomenological training. This could be encoded as a second-tier qualification requirement specific to M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL and any future M-CONTEMPLATIVE methodology.

### 3. The contemplative critique of concept-dependence

The deep contemplative insight — shared across Buddhist, Daoist, and Sufi traditions — is that conceptual thought is a mediating layer that both enables and distorts direct knowing. Concepts are maps. The map is not the territory. An uncritical reliance on propositional knowledge misses everything the map cannot represent.

For a propositional knowledge chain, this is a principled challenge: every claim the chain encodes is already a map. The chain should acknowledge this explicitly — not as a deficiency, but as a design constraint. Claims about phenomena that resist propositional capture (conscious experience, flow states, aesthetic insight, phenomenal qualia) are accessible to the chain only through imperfect linguistic description.

The correct response is not to try to encode the non-conceptual but to **encode the limits**: mark claims about first-person experience with an epistemological caveat that the encoded proposition is a description of, not a substitute for, the direct knowing it points toward. This is LOGIC-077 applied to phenomenological claims specifically: the chain knows its descriptions are incomplete. That's not a bug — it's the first step toward using them well.

### 4. Intersubjective verification as convergence criterion

Contemplative traditions developed their own version of intersubjectivity: the dharmic tradition requires that realized teachers' descriptions of meditative states converge with each other. If practitioners in different cultures, traditions, and centuries report similar phenomenology of jhāna states or non-dual awareness, this convergence is taken as evidence that they are reporting something real about the structure of mind rather than artifacts of their specific tradition.

This is analogous to scientific intersubjective agreement but applied to first-person data. The chain's M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL ComplianceCriteria requirement for "intersubjective corroboration by at least N independent phenomenologists" operationalizes exactly this — but misses the cross-tradition dimension. Corroboration from practitioners of different contemplative traditions is stronger evidence than corroboration from practitioners within a single tradition (who might share a common vocabulary that produces apparent agreement without genuine convergence).

**Architecture implication**: The qualification metadata for M-PHENOMENOLOGICAL verifiers should track not just training level but contemplative tradition. Cross-tradition convergence could then be scored as stronger evidence than within-tradition convergence.

### Priority assessment

**★★★★★ Human-only panels**: `required_verifier_types` on `Methodology`. One proto field. Three-line change in `GetEligibleValidators`. The auth layer already has the data. This is not only a contemplative-traditions gap — it is the correct infrastructure for any epistemological domain where the nature of the verifier matters (phenomenal consciousness, embodied knowledge, AI-verified mathematical proofs). The fix is small; the epistemological consequence is large.

**★★★★☆ ConsequenceInstance log** (from pragmatism brief) and **★★★☆☆ temporal verification window**: both address the temporal dimension. Claims about states that persist over time — contemplative realization, habit formation, pragmatic track records — need a verification protocol that extends across time, not a single-round verdict.

The deepest contribution of contemplative traditions to zerone's architecture is not any specific mechanism. It is the insistence that **the observer matters**. The chain already knows this in abstract — qualification weights exist precisely to gate who can verify what. Contemplative epistemology makes the principle concrete and extends it to a domain the chain currently underserves: the first-person structure of mind.
